**Figure 3 | Humans and frozen multimodal models share shape- and illumination-dependent gloss judgments.** Analyses use the existing mean reference-matching responses for all 3,888 stimuli (36 shapes × 36 illuminations × 3 viewpoints). Human responses are previously collected observer averages; each model value averages two complete matching runs. **a,b,** Adjusted effects for all 36 shapes (a) and illuminations (b), independently estimated for humans, InternVL and Qwen3. Conditions are ordered by the human effect; the grey line connects human estimates as a visual guide. Model symbols show the corresponding condition, without fitting model values to human values. Responses were standardized separately for each observer/model. An additive regression included categorical physical gloss, viewpoint, chroma, shape and illumination, and sine/cosine hue terms at the first two harmonics. Factor coefficients were centered across their 36 levels. Effects therefore denote departures from an observer/model's average conditional response, in its own response standard deviations. **c,** Spearman correlations between human and model condition effects: shape, 0.504 and 0.671; illumination, 0.809 and 0.799 (InternVL and Qwen3, respectively). Error bars indicate 95% condition-bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples). Two-sided condition-label permutation tests used 99,999 permutations; Benjamini–Hochberg correction across these four tests yielded q = 0.00177 for InternVL shape and q = 0.0000133 for each other comparison.

**d,e,** Representational dissimilarity matrices (RDMs) of full shape-response profiles (d) and illumination-response profiles (e), following physical/color adjustment. For each observer/model separately, nuisance regressions included categorical physical gloss and chroma and first/second hue harmonics; coefficients fitted on two viewpoints were applied to the third, rotating through all three viewpoints. Residual responses were averaged across viewpoints. Each shape profile contains responses across 36 illuminations; each illumination profile contains responses across 36 shapes. Dissimilarity is the mean squared difference between two profiles. Matrices show the same original condition-ID order for all observers/models, without coordinate alignment. For display only, each matrix is divided by its mean off-diagonal dissimilarity; colors saturate at three times that mean. These full profiles retain additive shape and illumination effects, unlike interaction-only residuals. **f,** All 630 distinct condition-pair distances for each human–model comparison, shown as fractional ranks. Each point is a pair of conditions, not an independent image or participant. Dashed diagonals mark equal distance ranks, not a fitted relationship. The four RSA coefficients are 0.327 and 0.475 for shape and 0.537 and 0.487 for illumination (InternVL and Qwen3, respectively). **g,** RSA for all three observer/model pairings, with 95% node-bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples of 36 condition nodes, excluding repeated-node diagonal pairs). Two-sided node-label permutation tests used 4,999 permutations, preserving matrix dependencies; q values were corrected across the original family of 18 raw, adjusted and interaction-only RSA comparisons. All six adjusted-profile comparisons shown here have q ≤ 0.0009. The complete original test family accompanies the source data.

**h,** Representative stimuli for the lowest and highest human-adjusted shape and illumination effects. Conditions were selected using human effects alone. Within each condition, three first-viewpoint images nearest the dataset median physical gloss were selected, with image ID breaking ties. Labels below the images give physical gloss c; colored values summarize the corresponding condition effects across the entire dataset, not the three illustrated images. Other stimulus properties vary between these photographs; this gallery illustrates the conditions rather than constituting a controlled image manipulation. Taken together, the figure supports shared conditional behavioral structure, particularly for illumination, rather than identifying model-internal neural dimensions. All uncertainty estimates concern sampled conditions, not participant-population uncertainty.
