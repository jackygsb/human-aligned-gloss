**Figure 4 | Gloss judgments transfer across rating, reference matching and pairwise choice.** **a,** Three behavioral interfaces and the cross-task prediction logic. Task questions are schematic summaries, not verbatim acquisition prompts. Continuous mappings are estimated between each frozen model's own outputs; human judgments are not used as calibration targets or predictive inputs. **b,c,** Out-of-fold predictions of reference-matched gloss from single-image ratings (b), and of single-image ratings from reference matches (c), for InternVL and Qwen3. Each plot contains 3,849 images from the matching-heldout cohort, excluding the original matching-development set. Monotonically increasing isotonic regression was fitted within each model using 12 pre-existing shape-group folds; all images from the test shapes were excluded from fitting that fold's calibration. Values outside the training predictor range were clipped to the fitted boundary. Dashed lines denote exact prediction; colors indicate the number of images per hexagon on a shared logarithmic scale. Horizontal bands reflect discrete source responses and the fitted monotonic mapping. The plotted R² values use all out-of-fold predictions, without fitting a rescaling to the test data.

**d,e,** Cross-validated R² for both mapping directions under shape-group and illumination-group holdout. Each split independently uses 12 folds. Rating-to-matching R² is 0.652/0.654 for InternVL and 0.776/0.778 for Qwen3 (shape/illumination holdout); matching-to-rating R² is 0.689/0.691 and 0.749/0.750, respectively. Error bars are 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 crossed shape–illumination bootstrap samples of the fixed out-of-fold predictions. Calibration is not refitted within these bootstrap samples; the intervals quantify stimulus-condition uncertainty conditional on the saved outputs and fitted mappings. This calibration is a post-processing analysis, not retraining of either multimodal model.

**f,** Agreement between the direction of each model's single-image rating or reference-matching difference and its independently collected two-image choices. All 2,000 canonical image pairs are included. Each pair was presented in both display orders; answers were mapped back to the same image identities and averaged, with explicit ties coded as 0.5. For a source-task difference Δ and canonical choice frequency p(A), agreement is 0.5 + sign(Δ)[p(A) − 0.5], with numerical ties in Δ receiving half credit. Rating ordering yields 76.70% and 80.35% agreement for InternVL and Qwen3; reference-matching ordering yields 79.65% and 85.075%. The horizontal reference line indicates 50%. No mapping is trained for these directional comparisons. Error bars are 95% image-node bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples; 2,419 unique endpoint images), weighting each pair by the product of its endpoint multiplicities and retaining both display orders together.

**g,** Empirical choice curves linking signed rating differences (left) and matched-gloss differences (right) to the observed frequency of choosing image A. Rating differences are divided by 100 and matching differences by 0.1487; ten fixed equal-width bins span [−1, 1]. Points show the mean normalized difference and mean choice frequency of all pairs in a nonempty bin. Symbol area increases with bin count (an additive minimum area preserves visibility); lines connect means and are not fitted psychometric functions. Binning is descriptive, with no inferred trial-level independence or confidence bands. **h,** Directional agreement for the 1,000 random pairs and 1,000 human–physical conflict pairs separately, using the same scoring and node-bootstrap procedure as f. Conflict pairs were selected from opposite human-mean and physical-gloss orderings in the existing pair manifest. Human information defines this pair stratum but does not enter the model-to-model cross-task predictor. The pair mixture is designed rather than a random sample of all possible image pairs.

**i,** Two illustrative random pairs for which both models agree across all three tasks. Among such concordant pairs with nonzero rating and matching differences, examples were selected nearest the 25th and 75th percentiles of InternVL's signed matching differences across all random pairs, breaking ties by pair ID. Tables show A − B differences and the observed choice frequency for A, averaged over display orders. These examples are explicitly selected illustrations; panels f–h retain all pairs, including task disagreements. Together, the figure supports cross-task consistency of model gloss judgments and transfer of calibration to held-out shape or illumination groups. It does not assume that all task outputs are interchangeable or identify a unique internal perceptual mechanism.
