**Figure 6 | Reference-defined perturbations of internal visual states bidirectionally change gloss choices.** **a,** Construction of a candidate direction from 52 reconstructed reference images spanning physical gloss c = 0–0.1487; five levels are illustrated. Each reference is passed through the frozen model under the single-image gloss prompt. Mean bridge-token activations are regressed coordinate-wise on reference level normalized to [0, 1]. The multivariate slope vector β defines direction d = β/‖β‖ and full-range scale s = ‖β‖. Increasing reference gloss determines the positive sign. Human test-image judgments do not enter this definition. **b,** Reference mean activation projected onto d, independently scaled to [0, 1] for display in each model. This is a construction check on the same reference sequence used to define d, not independent generalization evidence.

**c,** Original test image #64 and its native 16 × 16 bridge-token projection maps, h(p)·d, overlaid with alpha 0.48 and nearest-neighbor interpolation. Full-image minimum and maximum set the color limits separately in each model. These are raw directional projections, not attention weights, learned filter kernels or causal attribution maps. The illustration was previously selected by ranking the 72 intervention images by the smaller of the two models' spatial correlations with positive local brightness, inspecting the top eight, and choosing the third-ranked image for a clearly visible specular surface. It therefore illustrates spatial response rather than estimating population-level highlight selectivity. All arrays and the original selection audit accompany the source data. **d,** Additive intervention h′(p) = h(p) + αsd at every bridge token of the test image, using α ∈ {−1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1}. The same perturbation vector is applied uniformly; it is not restricted to highlighted regions in c. Pixels, model weights, prompt and reference tokens remain unchanged. Qwen3's final merger output is perturbed while its DeepStack paths remain intact. The assay uses 72 images covering 36 shapes, two viewpoints and illumination 1, each compared with reconstructed reference #27 in both display orders.

**e,f,** Change from the image/order-specific baseline in the test-glossier choice score for InternVL (e) and Qwen3 (f). Next-token logits are normalized over the verified answer tokens A, B and SAME; after mapping answer labels to test/reference roles, the score is P(test glossier) + 0.5P(SAME). Values are reported in percentage points (pp), not relative percentages, physical-gloss units or accuracy changes. Lines connect empirical dose means. Wine denotes the reference-defined direction; gold denotes a whole-image-luminance covariance direction; the other lines denote three seeded random directions orthogonal to d. All controls have equal perturbation norm at a given dose. The brightness control is a covariance-derived direction rather than a guaranteed pure luminance mechanism. Reference-direction bands show archived 95% shape-cluster bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples of 36 shapes), after averaging the two viewpoints and both presentation orders within shape. The change from α = −1 to +1 is 19.293 pp for InternVL and 20.118 pp for Qwen3. The positive endpoint changes are 9.919 and 10.249 pp, respectively. Zero-dose changes are identically zero by baseline definition.

**g,h,** Paired shape-level excess bidirectional response of the reference-defined direction over each control. For each shape, the contrast is [Sreference(+1) − Sreference(−1)] − [Scontrol(+1) − Scontrol(−1)], averaged over its two images and both display orders. Each faint dot denotes one of 36 shapes; diamonds and bars show means and paired 95% shape-bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples). Two-sided paired sign-randomization tests use 99,999 randomizations, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the eight model–control comparisons; all archived q values are 0.00001. The reference-versus-brightness excess is 14.457 pp for InternVL and 17.505 pp for Qwen3. Jitter is used only to separate plotted points and does not alter their values. **i,** Complete reference-direction dose-response matrices for all 36 shapes, in original shape-ID order. Each cell averages the two images and two display orders within shape; the common symmetric color scale preserves response magnitude and heterogeneity across models. The zero-dose row is defined by the unperturbed baseline.

The experiment establishes functional sensitivity to the reference-defined direction within this assay. The direction can encode entangled properties, and these additive perturbations need not remain on the natural activation manifold. The results do not identify an exclusive gloss circuit or establish intervention transfer to other illuminations. Unrestricted-token probability mass and individual raw assay outputs are retained in the source tables. The separate 12-image constrained-matching pilot is archived with the package but is not pooled into the 72-image primary assay or used in this figure's inference.
